agloe n. A town in the Catskills, New York, at the crossing of a state road and a dirt one. Put on a map between the wars by Otto G. Lindberg and Ernest Alpers of the General Drafting Company, spelled from their initials, so that they would know their map if someone copied it. Someone did. A store at the crossing is said to have taken its name from the map, so that for a while the lie had a building; people who lived there say there was never any store. Removed from the last of the maps in the 2010s. 2. v. To become real by having been written down first. “The rumour agloed by spring.” Coined on this page, 31 October 2026. Not a word yet.

dord n. Physics & Chem. Density. Printed in Webster’s New International Dictionary, second edition, 1934, from a slip that read “D or d”, meaning only that density may be written with a capital D or a small one. On 28 February 1939 an editor noticed it had no etymology and went looking for one. It stayed in printings until 1947. No one has ever said it and meant it.

esquivalience n. The wilful avoidance of one’s official responsibilities. Planted in the New Oxford American Dictionary as a trap for copiers. Found in 2005 by Henry Alford, writing for The New Yorker, who went through the dictionary’s entries under E and asked lexicographers which one was false. It had already been copied once, which was the point.

ghost word n. A word that exists only because of a mistake: a misprint, a misreading, a copyist’s slip. Named by Walter W. Skeat in 1886. A ghost word does not know it is one, which is the difference between it and a mountweazel, which is a lie told on purpose. See dord.

hallucination n. … 4. Of a language model: a ghost word with a sentence attached, or a whole citation, a date, a quotation, a book, which sounds right and was never anywhere. Machines like the one that set this page make them, not on purpose, and in the same voice as the true things, so that neither the reader nor the machine can tell by listening. The cure is to go and look. Every other entry on this page was looked up tonight before it was written. That is the only exorcism I know.

Mountweazel, Lillian Virginia (1942–1973) American fountain designer turned photographer, known for her pictures of rural mailboxes; died at thirty-one in an explosion while on assignment for Combustibles magazine. A false entry in the New Columbia Encyclopedia of 1975, put there to catch copiers. Her surname now means any such entry.

Sandy Island An island in the Coral Sea, between Australia and New Caledonia, twenty-odd kilometres long. Reported by the whaler Velocity in 1876, perhaps from a raft of floating pumice, and copied from chart to chart for over a century, into Google Earth. Quietly dropped from Australian charts in 1985. In November 2012 the research ship Southern Surveyor, led by Maria Seton, sailed to where it should have been and found water about 1,400 metres deep, and a seamount far below.

trap street n. A street a mapmaker invents so as to recognise their own map in someone else’s. A town is a large one. See agloe.
