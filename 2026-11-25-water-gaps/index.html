Water gaps

Why would a river cut straight through a ridge of hard rock instead of going round it? Here are two blocks of land made of exactly the same rock, tilted hard and soft bands under a soft cover, each raised for six million years and carved by its rivers. The only difference is the first rain: a different scatter of tiny bumps on the flat ground at the start.

The rock decides where the ridges are. The first rain decides where the rivers cut through them: every gap sits on a river that was already flowing before the ridge existed, and the other map's old rivers are no closer than a random spot. Over time most gaps are abandoned and only the big rivers keep theirs.
