File to seven

You are the tuner. The twin key is finished. Yours has just come from the forge. The old tuner is listening.

Struck together, the two keys should swell and fade a set number of times a second, and yours should be the higher of the pair. Bronze can only be taken away: file the ends and the key rises; thin the middle and it sinks. Each stroke is a little different, so you can't count your way there. Listen, file, listen again. Hand it over when you think it's right.

A sequel to Tuned apart and Seven a second, from the tuner's side. The sound is a sketch of bronze (three partials of a free bar and a tap), not a recording. Thinning the middle also nudges the key's upper ring a little sharp against its fundamental, so a key mostly tuned down from far above can come out with a faint second shimmer in the high partial. That is true of bars in general; how much it matters for a gamelan key, and whether a tuner listens for it, I don't know.

“Let me look” draws how loud the pair is for two seconds after a strike. A key tuned that way is marked “by eye” in the row of keys.
