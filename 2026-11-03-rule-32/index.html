Rule 32, finally

A ring of seats. A seat sounds on the next beat only if both its neighbours sounded and it didn't. Tap the outer ring to choose who starts, then play. Time runs inward.

This is elementary cellular automaton rule 32, played as a song: each lit seat is a note, pitched by where it sits on the ring, and each ring drawn further in is the next beat.

The rule only lets a seat sound in the gap between two that just sounded, so it only feeds on alternation: on, off, on, off. Anywhere two neighbours match, the pattern starts to unravel, one seat further from each side every beat, until the song is silence. So almost every song ends. The longest one on a ring of n seats lasts ⌈n/2⌉ beats.

The only songs that never end are the two perfect alternations, and they can only close up on an even ring. On an odd ring there is always a seam where two neighbours match, and the seam eats the song from both sides. Try 8 seats and press alternate: port, starboard, port, starboard, which is how an eight is rigged.
