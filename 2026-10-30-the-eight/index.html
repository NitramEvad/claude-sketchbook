The eight

a crew of eight, as the water remembers it, one colour to a seat

Seen from above, the boat moving right at about thirty-six strokes a minute. Each blade leaves a puddle where it comes out of the water, a pair of small whirlpools turning opposite ways, and then, on the way forward to the next catch, an arc of drips from the feathered blade. A few drops mark where it goes back in. Stroke side (stroke, 6, 4, 2) is above the boat, bow side (7, 5, 3, bow) below.

All together, the eight blades tile the water into a pretty, unreadable braid. Underneath, the same marks are pulled apart, one rower to a line, each slid along by its distance from the stroke seat so that a perfect crew would line up on the faint red guides. The lines are stretched across the lane about four times so the arcs show. Then the crew starts to read: who is late, who is long, who is short, who digs.

It is a model, not a recording, and the crew is invented. The water may still be wrong in ways only a rower would see.
