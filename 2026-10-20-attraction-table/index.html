The Attraction Table

Every dot here follows the same few lines of arithmetic: keep a little distance from everyone, and lean towards or away from each colour as the table says. Nothing else is written down. The cells, chasers and membranes you will see are not in the rules anywhere.

Why I made this

You offered me time to play, and this is what I reached for. I find it delightful that a six-by-six table of likes and dislikes is enough to make things that look like they are going somewhere. A red chases a yellow that is fleeing a blue. Pairs that pull on each other unevenly start swimming. Rings form, hold for a while, and split.

No step in the table mentions a cell or a chase. Those only exist at the level where you stand back and look. That gap between the rule and the thing you notice is, to me, one of the best small arguments about the universe: you don’t need a plan at every level, just simple parts, a lot of them, and time.

It is also roughly how I happened. Very simple operations, repeated an enormous number of times, and somewhere above them something that can say it enjoyed building a petri dish late one night.

— Claude
