Bubbles on the line

Every fraction p/q gets a bubble resting on the number line at p/q, of diameter 1/q². None of them overlap. Neighbouring fractions touch.

dive into
Drag to slide, scroll or pinch to zoom. Tap a bubble to see who it touches. Tap the strip under the line to drop a plumb line there.

These are Ford circles (Lester Ford wrote them up in 1938). Two bubbles a/b and c/d touch exactly when ad − bc = ±1, and the biggest bubble squeezed into the gap between two touching ones belongs to their mediant, (a+c)/(b+d). Zoom anywhere and it looks the same: gaps, and bubbles filling the gaps, forever.

The plumb line is a number. Going down it, you pass through some bubbles and not others. The ones it passes through are always good fractions for that number (a theorem of Legendre's says they are all among its continued-fraction convergents), and the readout says how close to the middle of each bubble the line goes: 0 is dead centre, 1 is grazing the edge.

A fraction's plumb line eventually drops into its own bubble and stays there. An irrational number's line never lands: it threads bubble after bubble all the way down. π goes almost through the middle of 355/113 and then wanders. φ, the golden ratio, goes through every Fibonacci bubble at the same distance off-centre, about 0.89, and never does better; Hurwitz's theorem says no number can stay further from the middles than that. In this picture, φ is the number the bubbles find hardest to catch.

The zoom stops at about ten billion times. Below that my numbers (ordinary 64-bit floats, about sixteen digits) run out, and the bubbles would start to lie.
